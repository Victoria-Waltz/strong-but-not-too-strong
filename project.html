The Project

The research behind Strong, But Not ‘Too Strong’.

Research Question

“What barriers, perceptions, and experiences shape women’s participation in public weight rooms?”

Project Goals

  • Explore the historical and cultural context of women’s strength training.
  • Examine common barriers women encounter in gym environments.
  • Discuss the relationship between fitness, confidence, and identity.
  • Consider the influence of social media and community.
  • Encourage thoughtful discussion about women’s experiences in fitness culture.

Interview and Research Material

Exploring Women’s Experiences in the Weight Room

As part of Strong, But Not ‘Too Strong,’ I explored the experiences and perspectives of approximately 20–25 college-aged women at Temple University, ranging from women who regularly lift weights to those with little or no weightlifting experience.

I wanted to understand not only why women choose to lift weights, but also what might prevent someone from getting started. By considering different levels of experience, I aimed to explore how confidence, social expectations, and gym environments influence women’s relationships with strength training.

Questions That Guided My Research

These guiding questions show what I wanted to learn about women’s experiences, challenges, and perceptions of weightlifting.

  1. Getting Started

    What initially motivated you to try weightlifting, or what has prevented you from starting?

  2. Gym Intimidation

    Have you ever felt intimidated or uncomfortable in a weight room? What contributed to that feeling?

  3. Confidence and Body Image

    How has weightlifting influenced your confidence or the way you view your body?

  4. Gender Stereotypes

    Do you think women face pressure to be fit or strong without looking “too muscular”?

  5. Social Media

    How has social media influenced your expectations about fitness, body image, or strength training?

  6. Community and Support

    How important is encouragement from friends, other women, or the gym community in making you feel comfortable?

  7. Advice for Beginners

    What would make weightlifting feel more approachable for women who are interested but don’t know where to begin?

Five Themes Explored Throughout the Project

These themes connect the questions behind the podcast to the project’s supporting research.

Gym Intimidation

For women who are new to weightlifting, unfamiliar equipment and workout routines can make the gym feel overwhelming, especially when they’re worried about being judged.

Confidence and Body Image

Strength training can build confidence through personal progress and achievement. Women may also face conflicting expectations about appearance and how muscular they should look.

Community and Support

Supportive friends, workout partners, and welcoming gym environments can help beginners feel more comfortable. Encouragement and easy-to-find guidance can make strength training feel more approachable.

History and Gender Stereotypes

Cultural expectations about femininity and muscularity have shaped how women who lift weights are perceived. The project looks at how those expectations still influence conversations about women’s strength.

Social Media

Fitness content can motivate women to try lifting, learn new exercises, and connect with others. Idealized images and comparisons can also create unrealistic expectations about appearance and progress.

What I Took Away From This Project

One of the most interesting things I took away from this project is that many women seem interested in weightlifting, even if they aren’t currently doing it.

I think a major barrier isn’t necessarily a lack of interest, but the fear of being judged or not knowing where to start.

Something as simple as walking into a weight room without knowing how to use the equipment can feel intimidating, especially when it seems like everyone else already knows what they’re doing.

This made me think more about the importance of creating gym environments where beginners feel comfortable asking questions, learning exercises, and making progress at their own pace.

You don’t have to know everything about weightlifting to belong in the weight room. Everyone starts somewhere.
